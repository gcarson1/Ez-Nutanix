# Decisions

Choices made where the brief left room, or where the brief and other inputs disagreed.

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Purple slide look on top of the line-based design system: purple (`--ink`) is the structural color, plus a restrained status palette. | The master prompt asks for the slide's purple look; `DESIGN-SYSTEM.md` is monochrome. The original build's `tokens.css` had already merged them this way, so it was kept. |
| 2 | Preact (about 10 KB) for the UI. | Worth the bytes for a stateful, many-module UI; the build is still far under the 2 MB budget. |
| 3 | The whole scenario lives in the URL hash; cluster settings use shared keys (`src/state/scenario.ts`) across Protection & Capacity and Scale-out. The Cluster & DSF Lab and the Failure Gauntlet use fixed clusters and keep only their own choices (`sc`, `cp`) in the link. | A presenter can send a link to an exact configuration, and the top-bar presets load a cluster size into Protection & Capacity. |
| 4 | "Prism Federation" is presented as the slide's label for multi-Prism-Central federation under Nutanix Central, not as a product name. It opens the Nutanix Central module on its Federation tab. | No Nutanix product is called "Prism Federation" (docs/research/E-platform-services.md, 1d). |
| 5 | "Any Bare Metal Linux" opens Cloud Native AOS with the bare-metal target selected. | The brief maps it to 9.3. |
| 6 | Landing pane to module transition uses the View Transitions API (the clicked pane grows into the module header); browsers without it, and reduced motion, navigate without animation. | Spatial continuity without a dependency. |
| 7 | Presenter mode is a side drawer (P), not a second window. | Works when screen-sharing a single window; a second-window view can be added later. |
| 8 | Built file is not committed (`dist/` is ignored). CI builds it and uploads it as an artifact. | Keeps diffs reviewable; the deliverable is reproducible with `npm run build`. |
| 9 | The reference slide screenshot is not in the repo, so slide colors were not re-sampled (see design-tokens.md). | It was never added. |
| 10 | Facts that could not be verified are left out or shown with version-dependent wording rather than shipped as guesses. Details and sources are in `docs/facts.md`. | Brief, section 11. |
| 11 | Every module, the Cluster & DSF Lab included, offers preset choices above one picture that autoplays and loops with a scrub bar; most add a caption and a few key numbers. There are no step, Reset or speed controls, no Related boxes, and no screens that imitate Prism. | The owner asked for modules that read at a glance, presets instead of knobs, looping animations with scrubbing, and widgets that explain topics rather than demo the product. The audit is in the PR that made the change. |
| 12 | No module has an Honest fit ("Where this has trade-offs") callout. Trade-offs that matter stay in the presenter Q&A. | The owner asked to remove them all, overriding the master prompt's requirement for one per module. |
| 13 | The NCM Cost tab follows one project, dev-test, running hot, and shows how NCM brings it back under budget: a Cost Governance budget alert, its savings recommendations, then Self-Service quota, approval and scheduler policies. Each mechanism is checked in `docs/facts.md` section 6. | The owner found the old per-project showback, which only warned, understated how NCM prevents overspend. |
| 14 | Every looping animation pauses at the end of each step for a tenth of the loop's length, at least 0.4 s and at most 1 s. Data Lens always auto-blocks; the alert-only choice is gone. | The owner found the animations and captions moved too fast, and asked for a short delay after each step of no more than 1 s, and for Data Lens to show only auto-block. |
| 15 | The Cluster & DSF Lab is one fixed cluster (4 nodes, 4 VMs and 3 drives per node, RF2) with five scenarios chosen above the picture: data placement, live migration, planned shutdown, VM failover and drive failure. A one-line caption above the picture says what is happening; the right-hand pane explains the chosen scenario and does not change while it plays. The engine still decides the data layout, where HA restarts each VM and where each re-created copy lands; the lab only paces those results. | The owner asked for a set cluster size, obvious scenario buttons above the picture, no constantly updating right pane, and almost no customization. |
| 16 | Protection & Capacity has three tabs, like Cloud Manager: Copies (RF2, RF3; follows the shared node count and RF, so the top-bar presets size it), Erasure coding (one strip on 8 nodes, RF2 4/1 or RF3 4/2) and Blocks and racks (Node only, Block or Rack, on 8 nodes in 4 blocks or 12 nodes in 3 racks). Node only shows 1 of 8 pieces with both copies in one block, close to the 1-in-7 odds of random placement, so the risk is not exaggerated. The EC-X saving is shown three ways: copies only, an illustrative half-cold blend, and the all-cold theoretical maximum. | The simplified Cluster & DSF Lab no longer showed EC-X or block and rack awareness, so they moved here as presets. |
| 17 | The landing has a "Don’t want the full stack?" band under the slide. It opens one module with two tabs: Nutanix with external storage (choose Everpure FlashArray, Dell PowerStore or Dell PowerFlex) and ESXi on Nutanix (stay on ESXi, or convert to AHV later). Each tab leads with three "Why customers choose it" cards that light up as the loop reaches them. NetApp ONTAP appears only in the presenter Q&A because it isn’t generally available. Every claim is checked in `docs/facts.md` sections 7 and 8. | The owner asked for a homepage section for customers who don’t want the whole stack, covering Nutanix with external storage and ESXi on Nutanix, with researched business value and a widget for each. |
| 18 | The Objects tab tiers old objects to a "Cheaper S3-compatible store" labeled "optional: AWS S3, Azure Blob, other S3 clouds"; the tiering caption says the store can be in the customer’s data center or a public cloud. It doesn’t name a second Nutanix Objects store as the target, because no Nutanix page says so (`docs/facts.md` section 9). | The owner asked whether Objects can tier without AWS and, since it can, to show Nutanix doing the tiering with the cloud as an optional integration, then to drop a "no AWS needed" label and keep only the optional line. |
| 19 | Cloud Native AOS tells one story instead of comparing stacks: a database on Amazon EKS gets Nutanix storage from an AOS pod on each worker, keeps two copies of every block, rides out a worker failure, and NDK snapshots the whole app and replicates it to another AWS region, while apps on NKP in the data center can fail over to EKS. "Kubernetes runs on" picks Amazon EKS or Bare metal (NKP Metal); bare metal's cross-site step is marked planned. The VM-based option is gone, so an old `on=vm` link opens the EKS view. Storage is EBS gp3, not local NVMe (`docs/facts.md` section 10). | The owner found the old layer-by-layer comparison unclear and couldn't tell what message it was meant to land. This replaces the brief's "VM-based AOS vs Cloud Native AOS" toggle (9.3). |
| 20 | The top bar shows Presets only on Protection & Capacity and Scale-out vs 3-tier, the modules that read the shared cluster size (the redesigned Failure Gauntlet uses a fixed cluster, see 21); a preset opens Protection & Capacity at that size. The home page and every other module have no Presets menu. | The owner asked to remove the presets dropdown from the home page, then to keep it off modules where it doesn't apply, such as NKP. |
| 21 | The Failure Gauntlet follows the Cluster & DSF Lab's pattern: one row of failure buttons, a step tracker and one-line caption above the picture (click a step to jump there), one compact row of 8 nodes in 4 blocks, a legend with only the marks in use, "Step k of n" instead of a clock, and a right-hand outcome pane that does not change while it plays. The RF2/RF3 choice appears only for Two nodes, the one failure where it changes the outcome. The engine still decides placement, HA restart targets, unavailable data and every rebuild target. | The owner found the old page cluttered and too fast: a racing clock, a confusing health-over-time chart below the picture, a large legend and too many options at the top. |
| 22 | After an internal documentation review, technical claims carry short qualifiers where they appear: "target" on RPOs, "typical" or "illustrative" on timings, "sizing guidance" on capacity math, release-scoped versions and limits, and a Compatibility Matrix pointer in tooltips. The footer disclaimer carries the reviewer's standard sentence instead of repeating it on every claim. | The owner asked for updated labels and small caveats but no major changes, because the explorer is a customer's first introduction, with deeper technical material to follow. |
| 23 | Module headers have no Copy link or PNG buttons; the address bar still holds the scenario. The home page has no Open the Cluster Lab button, and the Guided tour is a round button fixed to the bottom right of the screen, hidden while the tour runs; the footer leaves room for it. | The owner asked for a cleaner header and home page. |
| 24 | The Copies tab draws four pieces of data (three on a 3-node cluster), so RF2 shows 8 copies and RF3 shows 12 instead of 16 and 24. Pieces sit in overlapping pairs spread around the cluster, so the failed node (N2) holds two pieces and their missing copies are rebuilt from two different nodes at the same time. Shapes grow to fill the node boxes. With no node count in the link, RF2 opens on 3 nodes and RF3 on 5, the smallest cluster for each; the tab and the presets write the node count to the link even when it is the shared default of 8. The Blocks and racks tab keeps eight pieces, because its point is the odds of two copies sharing a block. | The owner asked for fewer shapes so replication and the copy-over after a failure are easy to follow, and for RF2 to show 3 nodes first. |
| 25 | The landing slide's public-cloud block shows only the providers' marks, as on the original slide: the AWS logo, the Azure "A" icon and the Google Cloud cloud icon, with no names beside them (each has alt text). They come from Wikimedia Commons; the Google Cloud icon is the colored cloud from its logo file without the wordmark. Sources are listed in `src/assets/logos/README.md`. They are used only to name where the platform runs. | The owner asked for the logos from the original slide, then for marks only, without the words Azure and Google Cloud. |
| 26 | Replaces 14: animations no longer pause between steps. Each step lasts at least 0.3 s per word of its caption (heading included); a step that would otherwise be shorter plays slower, and the speed eases into and out of it, so nothing freezes. The only hold is 0.6 s on the last frame before a loop restarts. Looping dots on links move at one calm speed (70 diagram units per second) whatever the link's length, rebuild copies and VM moves in the Lab and Gauntlet travel more slowly, and in Datapath and Flow's virtual networks the packet crosses at its normal pace and then waits while the caption is read. | The owner found the step pauses stuttery and many animations, captions and dots too fast, and asked for each step to get reading time while the animation keeps running, at least 0.3 s per word, smooth like the External storage and ESXi animations. |
| 27 | Version 1.1. The release is numbered in `package.json` (1.1.0); the footer shows "v1.1" and About says "Version 1.1". The file shared before this release is 1.0. Release notes are in `CHANGELOG.md`. | The owner asked for these changes to be the v1.1 rollout, with the previous file as 1.0. |
| 28 | Amends 11: next to play/pause, a speed button (YouTube's choices: 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, Normal, 1.25, 1.5, 1.75, 2) sets one playback speed for every animation, on top of the reading-time pacing in 26. It is remembered in this browser, and `<` and `>` step it. There are still no step or Reset controls. | The owner asked for a 1x button with a speed menu like YouTube's. |
| 29 | LCM Rolling Upgrade drops "Cluster already degraded" (the pre-checks are now a talk-track line). In "A node fails mid-upgrade" the timeline shows LCM's plan until the node fails, then the new plan: the failure marker, a hold that grows as it happens, and the repair marker only once reached. Data protection reads "Rebuilding" while the failed node is out, since the rebuild starts at once (LCM-1). | The owner asked to remove the degraded scenario and to make the failure come without warning, showing how the cluster holds and recovers. |
| 30 | Disaster Recovery offers Async, "NearSync / Sync" and Metro Availability. The shared choice shows NearSync's small recovery points and says in its captions what Sync changes; Metro is sync plus a Witness, with automatic failover that restarts the VMs, and a planned move that live-migrates them (DR-6 to DR-11). | The owner found NearSync and Sync looked the same and asked for one choice, plus a researched Metro Availability (under 5 ms RTT). |
| 31 | Edge & ROBO: "Nodes lose contact" works on two and three nodes (a single node has none to lose, and the button says so). On two nodes the Witness picks the node that carries on and the other waits while its VMs move; on three, the two connected nodes carry on (EDGE-4, EDGE-5). A new "Add a node" event shows a single node can't grow, two nodes become three from AOS 7.6, and three nodes use Expand Cluster (EDGE-6). | The owner reported the button never lit up and asked what expanding one- and two-node clusters looks like. |
| 32 | NetApp ONTAP is a fourth array on the external storage picker, over NFS. Until it is generally available, its numbers say "Announced" with the planned release, its note says to check availability, and its captions claim only what is published (NFS on Ethernet, AFF A-series and select FAS); the volume-per-disk, array snapshot and Prism storage-management lines are left out for it (EXT-15). | The owner asked for NetApp to be added. No general availability had been announced by October 22, 2026, so it is shown as announced rather than as shipping. |
| 33 | The footer credit reads "Built by Alex Besaw and Gabriel Carson", at 15 px semibold, larger than the disclaimer under it. | The owner asked for their names to be bigger and for Claude to be taken out of the credit. |
| 34 | Left and Right arrows pause the animation and move it a fortieth of the loop; Shift with an arrow jumps to the previous or next step (where the caption changes). On the scrub bar itself the arrows keep the bar's own finer steps. Still no step or Reset buttons. | The owner asked for the arrow keys to scrub through animations in increments. |
| 35 | Amends 33: the credit is a small "Built by" label, then one column per author from `config.json`: the name, and under it the Slack handle (@Besaw, @GabeC) in Nutanix purple (`--ink`, #7855fa in dark), with "on Slack" once, after the last handle. Columns replace the "Built by Alex Besaw and Gabriel Carson" sentence, where the handles floated under the names at uneven offsets. The version moved to the right, beside About. On the home page the slide fills the space between the top bar and the footer instead of the window height, so when the slide fits, the footer sits at the bottom of the window with no scrolling. | The owner asked for the handles, and for the footer to show without scrolling when there is room. |
| 36 | Amends 17 and 32: the external storage tab has no array picker. A "Works with" list names the arrays and nothing else: Everpure FlashArray, Dell PowerStore · PowerFlex (Dell once), NetApp ONTAP. NetApp's announced status is in the numbers under the picture ("4 supported arrays, 3 generally available, NetApp announced") and the presenter notes. The loop is the same for every array ("Your Everpure / Dell / NetApp array", Ethernet with NVMe/TCP, Dell SDC or NFS), and the per-disk volume and snapshot captions say they apply to the generally available arrays. | The owner wanted the visuals to lead and the support list to be clear without making viewers pick an array for a scenario, with names only and no extra detail in the list. |
| 37 | Amends the brief's no-runtime-dependencies rule: icons come from Lucide (lucide-preact, ISC license, notice in the About dialog), drawn at our 1.75 stroke through the existing `Icon` component, so every icon changes in one place. Polish stays inside the design system: landing tiles get a soft top-lit sheen and a hairline highlight, diagrams (not tables) sit on a faint dot grid, node cards in diagrams have 6px corners (VMs, CVMs and drives 3px), the Explore grid draws its hairlines on the tiles so empty last-row cells stay page-colored, and the node cutaway's "drives passed to the CVM" label wraps instead of being clipped. | The owner asked for more polished icons and shapes for 2026 from the awesome-cc0 list. That list holds 3D models, game packs, photo textures and sound, nothing that suits a line-based UI, so the owner chose a permissively licensed UI icon set instead. |
| 38 | Experimental, on the `liquid-glass-home` branch only, not for main: the home slide's tiles are liquid glass rendered by liquidGL 3.0 (WebGPU/WebGL, MIT, notice in About), over a purple and blue gradient backdrop that is the only thing the glass refracts. The "Nutanix Central" band is a lower glass layer its tiles stack on. liquidGL's tint can only darken, so each tile keeps its brand color as a translucent fill drawn over clear glass, plus a light rim and inner highlight; all home-page text measures at least 5.2:1. Tile text stays in the DOM above the glass. The glass starts only on a hardware GPU, never inside another page's frame, and not with reduced transparency or forced colors; otherwise the tiles look as on main. It costs frame time (about 45 to 48 fps instead of 60 on an Intel Iris laptop). It departs from the design system's quiet, line-based look, on the home page only. | The owner asked for liquid glass on the home page UI, like their reference images, using the best library for high-quality glass, and chose liquidGL (WebGL) over a CSS/SVG build and liquid-glass-js. |
